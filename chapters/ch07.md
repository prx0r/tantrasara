Now, you might be asking:

“Abhinava, mate, what’s the point of learning all this?”

Excellent question.

Because we’ve spent a considerable amount of time naming worlds, gods, principles, cosmic eggs and their relative dimensions.

And if this were merely a geography lesson, you’d have every right to complain.

But it’s not.

We’ve been constructing a contemplative map.

And now we’re going to do something remarkable with it.

We’re going to dissolve the whole bloody thing.

15. The entire cosmos returns to its source
Remember the beginning of the chapter?

The great process of dissolution?

All the cosmic paths within the body.

The body within prāṇa.

Prāṇa within the intellect.

The intellect within the void.

And the void within consciousness.

Well, now you actually understand what we’re dissolving.

Every one of those 118 worlds.

Every presiding deity.

Every tattva.

Every cosmic egg.

Every apparent spatial distinction.

Everything.

The entirety of:

Deśādhva.

The path of space.

Now, imagine you’ve completed this enormous study.

You know the hierarchy.

You understand the relationship between the principles.

You see how each higher principle pervades the lower.

And you recognise that even the highest enumerated categories are not independent of Śiva.

Now contemplate the entire cosmic path within your own body.

Then the body in prāṇa.

Then prāṇa in the intellect.

Then the intellect in the great openness.

Then even that openness in consciousness.

And finally, recognise that consciousness as the supreme reality.

Paramaśiva.

Now, don’t imagine that the universe has physically disappeared.

The point isn’t that someone has switched off the stars.

It’s that the apparent independence of the entire spatial manifestation is being relinquished.

All the differences remain possible as manifestations.

But their underlying reality is recognised as consciousness.

And now, because that consciousness is both transcendent and immanent, the universe does not need to be rejected.

You don’t have to run away from the world.

You don’t have to deny the existence of trees, mountains, people or divine forms.

You recognise their underlying conscious reality.

And this brings us to the concluding verse.

16. The final verse — you’re not a tiny thing inside an enormous universe
ORIGINAL SANSKRIT

अध्वानमिमं सकलं देहे प्राणेऽथ धियि महानभसि।संविदि च परं पश्यन्पूर्णत्वाद्भैरवीभवति॥

Adhvānam imaṃ sakalaṃ dehe prāṇe ’tha dhiyi mahānabhasi.

Saṃvidi ca paraṃ paśyan pūrṇatvād bhairavībhavati.

Right.

Let’s finish.

See the entire cosmic path.

Adhvānam imaṃ sakalam.

All of it.

All those worlds.

All those cosmic principles.

Every appearance.

Contemplate it in the body.

Dehe.

Then in prāṇa.

Prāṇe.

Then in the intellect.

Dhiyi.

Then in the great space.

Mahānabhasi.

And in consciousness.

Saṃvidi.

Now, the crucial thing is how you’re seeing it.

You’re apprehending the entire path as:

Param.

Supreme.

Not as something ultimately separate from the divine.

Not as a collection of foreign objects standing outside consciousness.

But as grounded in the supreme reality itself.

And through that recognition of fullness:

Pūrṇatvāt.

What happens?

Bhairavībhavati.

One becomes Bhairava.

Now, that’s a magnificent ending.

Because we began with a problem.

The world looks absolutely enormous.

You appear tiny.

A little individual occupying a particular position in space.

And the entire universe seems to surround you.

So we investigated the structure of spatial manifestation.

We discovered an enormous hierarchy of worlds and principles.

We ascended through them.

And at every level we found a more comprehensive principle pervading the preceding one.

Until we arrived at Śiva.

The immeasurable consciousness that pervades and transcends all the cosmic paths.

And then we recognised that this consciousness isn’t something alien to us.

It’s the very ground of our own awareness.

So the mistake was never that the universe appeared vast.

The mistake was assuming that this vastness existed independently of consciousness.

You thought you were a tiny conscious thing inside an enormous universe.

Abhinavagupta is teaching you to recognise the universe as an appearance within the unrestricted reality of consciousness.

And that reality?

Paramaśiva.

Now, notice something especially beautiful.

Nothing has to be destroyed.

Not the body.

Not the world.

Not the gods.

Not the different realms of existence.

The hierarchy of manifestation is not an unfortunate accident.

It’s the expression of Śakti’s own power to manifest diversity.

Mūrti-vaicitrya-ābhāsana-śakti.

The very power we began with.

So the chapter has returned to its beginning.

The universe rests in consciousness.

The individual comes to recognise that reality through systematic understanding and contemplative dissolution.

And that recognition is the state of Bhairava.

Bhairavībhāva.

Rather extraordinary, isn’t it?

We’ve just travelled through innumerable universes, listed 118 divine worlds, passed beyond every measurable cosmic principle, and somehow ended up exactly where we started.

In awareness.

The whole bloody universe was never outside Śiva in the first place.
