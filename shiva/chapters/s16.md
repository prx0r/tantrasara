# Śiva Sūtras 1.16

शुद्धतत्त्वसन्धानाद्वापशुशक्तिः

Śuddhatattvasandhānād vāpaśuśaktiḥ.

Through union with the pure principle, the power of bondage associated with the limited being is absent.

You Are No Longer a Bound Little Animal.

Right.

We have to talk about paśu.

Because in Śaiva terminology, paśu is the bound individual.

Literally, the word can mean animal or beast.

But don’t misunderstand this as an insult to animals.

It’s a technical designation for the individual limited by the bonds called pāśa.

The paśu experiences itself as separate, incomplete, and constrained.

And the tradition contrasts this with Pati — the Lord.

The bound being and the Lord.

Now, if Śiva were fundamentally separate from the individual, you’d have a terrible philosophical problem.

How does a finite being ever become truly infinite?

How does something inherently limited acquire the nature of the limitless?

You can’t simply glue infinity onto a finite object.

Very messy business.

But Trika’s answer is different.

The limitation is a contraction within the manifestation of consciousness.

It is not the ultimate nature of consciousness.

Through śuddhatattvasandhāna, union with the pure principle — identified by Kṣemarāja with Paramaśiva — the binding power no longer governs the yogi’s identity.

And notice the word vā.

“Or.”

Kṣemarāja introduces this as another means of understanding the attainment described previously.

The yogi recognises the world as inseparable from the pure reality of Śiva.

The power of paśutva, bound individuality, is overcome.

In the language of recognition:

You do not turn a fundamentally non-Śiva entity into Śiva.

You recognise that the apparently limited individual was never ultimately separate from Śiva.

Sanskrit & Trika Shaivism.

The chains weren’t another cosmic being’s property.

They were part of the Lord’s own self-concealment.

And now the self-concealment is being recognised.
