# Śiva Sūtras 1.3

योनिवर्गः कलाशरीरम्

Yonivargaḥ kalāśarīram.

The source and its multiplicity; that whose nature is limited activity.

The Universe Has Three Different Ways to Ruin Your Day.

Vasugupta now expands the explanation of bondage.

The sūtra is extremely compact. Kṣemarāja reads it in connection with the previous statement, “Knowledge is bondage,” and elaborates the remaining forms of impurity.

Welcome to the three malas.

Āṇavamala — The impurity of smallness.

The fundamental feeling of incompleteness.

“I am only this.”

“I am not enough.”

“I am this little subject rather than the limitless whole.”

The infinite has apparently decided to identify as a fraction of itself.

Māyīyamala — The impurity of differentiation.

Now consciousness doesn’t merely experience itself as limited.

It experiences reality as divided into separate objects.

This is mine.

That’s yours.

That’s pleasant.

That’s disgusting.

That person disagrees with me and must therefore be metaphysically defective.

The world appears as a multiplicity of independently existing things.

Kārmamala — The impurity of limited agency and action.

Now the individual acts within the limitations it experiences, generating further karmic involvement.

“I did this.”

“I must obtain that.”

“I have to correct what happened yesterday.”

And the entire machinery of conditioned experience keeps operating.

Notice the elegance.

First, I am limited.

Then, the world is separate from me.

Then, I am a limited agent who must act within this separate world.

And suddenly you’ve got the entire human predicament.

The technical details of yoni, varga, and kalāśarīra belong to Trika’s theory of manifestation and its limited powers of agency, not merely a psychological metaphor. Kṣemarāja explicitly connects the sūtra with māyīyamala and kārmamala. Sanskrit & Trika Shaivism.
