# Śiva Sūtras 1.19

शक्तिसन्धाने शरीरोत्पत्तिः

Śaktisandhāne śarīrotpattiḥ.

Through union with Śakti, there is the production of bodies.

Oh Brilliant, Apparently You Can Create Bodies Now.

Right.

Well.

We’ve had a lovely philosophical discussion about consciousness.

We’ve examined bondage, recognition, meditation, the three states, the divine powers, and the unity of the universe.

And Vasugupta has now decided that we’re going to start creating bodies.

Of course we are.

This is Tantra.

At some point, the metaphysics was always going to become absolutely outrageous.

First, the traditional explanation.

Kṣemarāja links this directly to the icchāśakti described in 1.13.

Through profound union with divine will, the realised yogi is said to gain the capacity to manifest a body according to desire.

This is part of what traditional texts call vibhūti or siddhi — extraordinary spiritual powers.

And Kṣemarāja really does interpret the statement as a supernatural accomplishment. He is not merely describing ordinary creativity, imagination, or a poetic change in self-image. Sanskrit & Trika Shaivism.

So we shouldn’t pretend the text says something modest just because the literal claim makes modern readers uncomfortable.

At the same time, this is a traditional metaphysical and yogic claim, not an experimentally demonstrated human capacity.

Now why would such a claim appear here?

Because the entire first section has been describing a transformation from contracted agency to identification with universal consciousness.

In ordinary life, our capacity to act is restricted.

We can imagine countless things that we cannot actually produce.

But divine svātantrya, in the Trika system, is not restricted by those ordinary limitations.

The siddhi claims are expressions of that metaphysical distinction.

You can follow the philosophical logic without assuming that a contemporary practitioner can literally conjure an additional human organism into existence.

And perhaps the funniest thing is that this apparently astonishing power receives only one sūtra.

Vasugupta mentions the creation of bodies.

Then immediately moves on.

As though he’s just told you to remember to buy milk.
