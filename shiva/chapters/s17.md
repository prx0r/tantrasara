# Śiva Sūtras 1.17

वितर्क आत्मज्ञानम्

Vitarka ātmajñānam.

Reflective ascertainment is knowledge of the Self.

Your New Thought Is “I AM ŚIVA”, but Please Don’t Become Insufferable.

Now this one’s particularly interesting because we’ve just spent half the text discussing how conceptual knowledge can constitute bondage.

And suddenly Vasugupta says a form of reflective understanding is Self-knowledge.

WHAT?

I thought thinking was the problem.

Apparently we’re thinking again?

The crucial distinction concerns what the thinking reveals.

Kṣemarāja glosses vitarka here as the firm reflection:

Viśvātmā śiva evāsmi.

“I am indeed Śiva, whose Self is the universe.”

This is Kṣemarāja’s explanatory Sanskrit, rather than an additional Śiva Sūtra. Sanskrit & Trika Shaivism.

The statement is extraordinary.

The limited person ordinarily thinks:

“I am this particular organism.”

“I am this mind.”

“I am separate from the rest of existence.”

But now understanding reverses the structure of identification.

The yogi recognises their deepest identity with the consciousness whose manifestation is the universe.

And importantly, this does not license the individual ego to proclaim unlimited personal authority.

It isn’t:

“My personal opinions are infallible because I’m God.”

That’s merely the old limited ego putting on a particularly extravagant crown.

Rather, the point is that the fundamental subject is no longer mistaken for the contracted personality.

The recognition is not the inflation of the little self.

It is the recognition that the little self was never the complete Self.

And here’s the beautiful philosophical reversal.

Earlier:

Jñānaṃ bandhaḥ.

Knowledge is bondage.

Now:

Vitarka ātmajñānam.

A particular form of reflective understanding is knowledge of the Self.

Knowledge that reinforces limitation binds; understanding that discloses one’s universal nature liberates.

Same broad territory of cognition.

Completely different orientation.

It’s almost as though the problem wasn’t thinking.

It was misunderstanding what you are.
