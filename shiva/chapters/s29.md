# Śiva Sūtras 2.7

मातृकाचक्रसम्बोधः

Mātṛkācakrasambodhaḥ.

Complete awakening to the circle of Mātṛkā.

The Entire Sanskrit Alphabet Is a Secret Diagram of the Universe.

Right.

Now we’re getting into the properly beautiful stuff.

This is one of the most philosophically rich sūtras of the entire second section.

And Kṣemarāja’s commentary goes absolutely nuclear.

We had three words.

Mātṛkā.

Cakra.

Sambodha.

And he responds with an extraordinary exposition of the entire Sanskrit phonemic system as a manifestation of divine consciousness.

Apparently the alphabet is not just the alphabet.

It’s a metaphysical procession of Śakti.

Let’s unpack this.

Mātṛkā — the little mother; here, the divine matrix of phonemic powers.

Cakra — wheel, circle, collective.

Sambodha — complete awakening, full understanding or recognition.

In the preceding sūtra, the guru was introduced as the means.

Now we learn what genuine instruction can disclose.

The practitioner awakens to the true nature of Mātṛkā.

And this is where the entire discussion comes full circle.

Remember 1.4?

Jñānādhiṣṭhānaṃ mātṛkā.

Mātṛkā is the foundation of differentiated knowledge.

Earlier, we encountered her as a power associated with bondage.

Now we’re encountering the exact same power as a vehicle of enlightenment.

Why?

Because the letters are no longer apprehended merely as differentiated expressions concealing their source.

They are recognised as expressions of supreme consciousness.

And Kṣemarāja unpacks this through a detailed tantric phonology.

Let’s begin.

अ — A

The first vowel.

Kṣemarāja associates a with Anuttara.

The unsurpassable.

That beyond which nothing is higher.

The supreme reality.

Not simply a thing at the beginning of a sequence, but the fundamental source through which the entire sequence can be understood.

The alphabet begins with the unsurpassable.

Very ambitious for a vowel.

आ — Ā

The extended vowel.

Associated with Ānanda.

Bliss.

The supreme reality understood through its own fullness and delight.

So the movement from a to ā is not simply interpreted as a longer sound.

It is given a symbolic meaning within the unfolding of consciousness.

The unsurpassable and its bliss.

इ — I, and ई — Ī

Now we encounter icchā, will, and īśana, sovereignty or lordly power.

The divine doesn’t merely exist as an inert absolute.

It possesses the freedom to manifest.

This is absolutely fundamental to Trika.

If ultimate consciousness were entirely passive, we would have difficulty explaining manifestation as an expression of its own nature.

But Śiva is inseparable from Śakti.

And divine will belongs to that freedom.

उ — U, and ऊ — Ū

Now Kṣemarāja connects the phonemic unfolding with unmeṣa, opening or emergence, and ūnatā, a further differentiation associated with the developing appearance of objectivity.

The movement of manifestation becomes increasingly articulated.

This is not ordinary physical chronology.

We’re not claiming somebody pronounced a, then ā, and three seconds later the first galaxy appeared.

It is a symbolic, metaphysical account of the articulation of consciousness.

The vowels represent powers through which manifestation is understood.

And the sequence continues.

ऋ, ॠ, ऌ, ॡ — ṛ, ṝ, ḷ, ḹ.

These are connected with further differentiations involving the powers and their relations to elemental principles.

ए — E is associated with a particular union involving the powers symbolised by a and i.

ओ — O is associated with the corresponding union involving a and u, connected with the emergence of the power of action.

ऐ — Ai and औ — Au carry further symbolic meanings through combinations of those powers, including the integration of will, knowledge and action.

Then come the particularly important signs:

अं — Aṃ.

Bindu.

The point of concentrated unity.

And:

अः — Aḥ.

Visarga.

The power of emission.

Now things become extraordinarily interesting.

Because the alphabet is associated with the movement between undivided consciousness and the manifestation of differentiated reality.

The consonants then receive their own metaphysical interpretations.

The five consonantal classes, from ka through ma, are correlated with principles of manifestation.

The semivowels:

य र ल व — Ya, ra, la, va

are associated with further intermediate levels, including the limiting powers.

The sibilants and ha are related to the higher movement between difference and non-difference.

This is a structured tantric symbolism, not the ordinary linguistic explanation of how the Sanskrit alphabet historically developed.

And then Kṣemarāja introduces an absolutely magnificent idea.

The Mahāmantra.

The great mantra of I-consciousness.

अहम् — Aham.

“I.”

Now think about this.

The beginning of the alphabet.

A.

The unfolding powers of Śakti.

The sequence extending to ha and the concluding nasal element.

The tradition reads this totality symbolically in the great expression:

Aham.

I.

But not merely the ordinary personal pronoun.

Not the limited ego saying:

“I am hungry.”

“I am tired.”

“I am better at metaphysics than my neighbour.”

This is pūrṇāhantā.

Full I-consciousness.

The universal Self-awareness of Śiva.

The indivisible consciousness in which the entire manifest universe is included.

The great mantra is not merely a word used by an individual to refer to themselves.

It is an expression of the fundamental self-awareness of reality.

And now we can see the extraordinary elegance of the entire second section.

At 2.1:

The mind is mantra.

At 2.3:

The secret of mantra is its living divine consciousness.

At 2.7:

The entire matrix of phonemic powers is recognised as a manifestation of that consciousness.

And the culmination is Aham.

Not the small, contracted “I”.

But the universal I-consciousness in which every sound, every object, every cognition, and every form of manifestation is included.

Kṣemarāja’s extensive discussion here draws on the phonemic cosmology of the Parātriṃśikā tradition and the teachings developed by Abhinavagupta. It’s far more elaborate than the three-word sūtra alone, but entirely relevant to the reading we’re following.

And now, having realised that the alphabet is a manifestation of the Goddess, we’re ready for the next instruction.

Which is, apparently:

Throw your body into a fire.

Excellent.

Another perfectly normal day in Kashmir.
