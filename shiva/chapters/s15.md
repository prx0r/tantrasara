# Śiva Sūtras 1.15

हृदये चित्तसङ्घट्टाद्दृश्यस्वापदर्शनम्

Hṛdaye cittasaṅghaṭṭād dṛśyasvāpadarśanam.

Through the merging of mind into the Heart, the true nature of the seen and the state of apparent void become visible.

Put Your Mind Back Where It Came From, You Lunatic.

Now Vasugupta is going to explain how the extraordinary recognition described in the previous sūtra becomes possible.

And the key word is hṛdaya.

The Heart.

Not merely the muscular organ pumping blood around your chest.

The Heart in Trika is the centre, the innermost reality of consciousness.

The source from which manifestation arises.

Everything you’ve ever experienced has appeared in awareness.

Every sound.

Every image.

Every thought.

Every memory.

But ordinary attention continually moves towards the things appearing.

A noise appears.

You follow the noise.

A thought appears.

You follow the thought.

A sensation appears.

You construct a story about the sensation.

Before long, you’ve become entirely occupied with the contents of consciousness while ignoring the reality in which those contents appear.

The instruction involves cittasaṅghaṭṭa — bringing the mind into union with the Heart.

Instead of merely following the succession of objects, awareness becomes established in its own conscious ground.

And now something fascinating happens.

Dṛśya — the seen.

Svāpa — the condition of apparent absence or void.

Both are recognised in their underlying reality.

Presence and absence.

Manifestation and apparent emptiness.

The appearance of a universe and the disappearance of its differentiated contents.

Neither is ultimately independent of consciousness.

And this is a genuinely subtle point.

Because you might assume that the way to find consciousness is to remove every possible experience.

Close your eyes.

Stop thinking.

Become absolutely still.

Find a blank space.

And declare victory.

But Kṣemarāja doesn’t simply identify the void with the ultimate.

He describes the recognition of both manifested objects and the apparent void through their unity in conscious reality. Sanskrit & Trika Shaivism.

The point is not that consciousness is a special object hiding behind experience.

Nor is it a blankness that replaces experience.

It is the fundamental reality through which both appearing and disappearing are possible.

You’re not trying to manufacture the Heart.

You’re recognising what was already fundamental.
