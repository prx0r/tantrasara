# Śiva Sūtras 1.14

दृश्यं शरीरम्

Dṛśyaṃ śarīram.

The seen is the body.

Your Body Is the Entire Bloody Universe.

Oh, this one’s magnificent.

Dṛśya — that which is seen or perceived.

Śarīra — body.

Vasugupta is saying that for the realised yogi, the entire field of perceptible manifestation is experienced as their body.

Not merely the collection of flesh and bones they happen to be inhabiting.

Everything.

The trees.

The rivers.

The mountains.

The sky.

Other people.

The entire manifest universe.

Now, before you go outside shouting, “THAT BUS IS MY LEFT ARM,” we need to understand the metaphysical claim.

Your ordinary body is experienced as intimately belonging to you.

Your hand does not ordinarily appear as a completely foreign object with which you have no relationship.

You experience it as part of your embodied existence.

For the liberated yogi, Kṣemarāja describes an analogous but vastly expanded identification.

The whole field of manifestation is recognised as inseparable from the universal consciousness that is one’s true nature.

This isn’t a claim that your individual organism physically extends to every planet.

Nor does it mean other persons cease to have their own lived perspectives.

It is a radical transformation of the fundamental distinction between self and world.

What was previously experienced as alien and external is recognised as manifestation within the one conscious reality.

And Kṣemarāja explicitly includes both internal and external appearances.

The physical body, too, is something perceived.

It is not the ultimate perceiver.

Which means that your ordinary body is itself part of the manifested field.

Not a prison containing consciousness.

An expression of consciousness.

The universe has become the body of Śiva.

And the realised yogi recognises it as such.

Sanskrit & Trika Shaivism.
