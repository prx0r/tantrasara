# Śiva Sūtras 1.2

ज्ञानं बन्धः

Jñānaṃ bandhaḥ.

Knowledge is bondage.

Congratulations, Your Knowledge Is the Problem.

Oh, brilliant.

We’ve gone from “you are infinite consciousness” to “knowledge is your prison”.

Thanks, Vasugupta. Shall I cancel university?

Not quite.

Here jñāna means limited, differentiated knowing — knowledge structured around the fundamental division:

I am here. That thing is over there.

You see a tree.

There is you, the perceiver, and the tree, the perceived.

You immediately organise reality into categories.

Me. Tree. Other people. My possessions. My memories. My terribly important opinions.

Now ordinary differentiated knowledge is useful. It stops you walking into trees.

But the problem begins when this limited standpoint is mistaken for your complete identity.

Kṣemarāja associates this with āṇavamala, the primordial contraction in which universal consciousness experiences itself as a limited individual.

Mala means impurity, but don’t imagine literal dirt covering a perfectly clean soul.

Think of it as a fundamental limitation of awareness.

The infinite experiences itself as finite.

The complete experiences itself as incomplete.

The universally free experiences itself as somebody who desperately needs everything to work out a particular way.

And here’s the extraordinary move.

From the Trika perspective, bondage doesn’t require a second power to capture Śiva.

Śiva’s own freedom makes contraction possible.

The prisoner, the prison, and the authority permitting the imprisonment are not ultimately three independent entities.

It’s Śiva.

Doing the entire performance himself.

The philosophical point: Bondage is not knowledge as such, but the limiting mode of knowing which obscures one’s undivided nature. Kṣemarāja also connects bondage to the failure to recognise nondual awareness.
