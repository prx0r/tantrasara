# Śiva Sūtras 1.9

स्वप्नो विकल्पाः

Svapno vikalpāḥ.

Dreaming consists of conceptual constructions.

Dreams Are Your Mind Making Stuff Up.

Vikalpa is a hugely important word.

It means conceptual construction, differentiation, imagination, or determinate mental representation, depending on context.

Your mind has an extraordinary ability.

It can construct an entire experienced world without corresponding external sensory objects.

You fall asleep.

Suddenly you’re late for a mathematics exam.

You’re naked.

Your childhood friend has become the prime minister of Bulgaria.

And none of this seems particularly unusual.

The mind is constructing appearances.

But Trika’s observation extends beyond sleeping dreams.

Much of ordinary waking experience is also shaped by conceptual constructions.

You remember an argument.

You imagine tomorrow.

You rehearse a conversation that hasn’t happened.

You construct an entire future in which someone rejects you.

Then you get upset about it.

The other person hasn’t even entered the imaginary conversation yet.

You’ve invented them, invented their words, invented your response, and somehow come out of the encounter emotionally exhausted.

Congratulations.

You’ve been defeated by your own screenplay.

The crucial distinction is that Vasugupta is classifying modes of experience, not claiming that waking sensory perception and dreams are empirically identical.

But the same manifesting consciousness is involved in both.
