# Śiva Sūtras 1.11

त्रितयभोक्ता वीरेशः

Tritayabhoktā vīreśaḥ.

The experiencer of the three is the lord of heroes.

Congratulations, You’re Now the Lord of the Three Worlds.

We’ve now moved through waking, dreaming and deep sleep.

And Vasugupta asks an obvious question.

What is the condition of someone who has realised turya throughout these three states?

The answer:

Vīreśa.

Lord of the heroes.

Now that’s a title.

No certificate.

No embossed membership card.

Just Lord of the Heroes.

The word vīra means hero, but in the tantric context it can also relate to the powers of the senses.

Kṣemarāja interprets the realised yogi as sovereign over these powers, enjoying the conscious reality underlying the three conditions.

Why sovereignty?

Because such a yogi no longer fundamentally mistakes the passing forms of experience for separate, self-existing authorities.

Waking occurs.

Dreaming occurs.

Deep sleep occurs.

The underlying conscious reality is not diminished by any of them.

There is an enormous philosophical difference between being absorbed into an experience and recognising the source through which it appears.

An ordinary person becomes caught up in the drama.

The realised person can participate without losing the recognition of their deeper nature.

And notice that the text says bhoktā — experiencer or enjoyer.

Not somebody permanently fleeing experience.

Not a cosmic hermit refusing all manifestation.

The world continues to appear.

But its meaning has changed.

The hero doesn’t necessarily stop watching the performance.

The hero understands the nature of the theatre.
