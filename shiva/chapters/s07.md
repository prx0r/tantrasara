# Śiva Sūtras 1.7

जाग्रत्स्वप्नसुषुप्तभेदे तुर्याभोगसम्भवः

Jāgratsvapnasuṣuptabhede turyābhogasambhavaḥ.

In the distinctions of waking, dreaming and deep sleep, the experience of the fourth is possible.

Turns Out You’re Supposed to Be Awake While You’re Asleep.

Now Vasugupta introduces a magnificent idea.

The ordinary person experiences three principal states.

Jāgrat — waking.

Svapna — dreaming.

Suṣupti — deep sleep.

You wake up.

There’s a world.

You go to sleep.

There’s another world.

You fall into dreamless sleep.

Apparently, there’s no world at all.

Then you wake up again and go, “Bloody hell, where have eight hours gone?”

But across these changing conditions, the tradition introduces a fourth principle.

Turya.

Literally, “the fourth”.

A beautifully understated name for something of such metaphysical significance.

Didn’t call it the Great Golden Celestial Palace of Eternal Transcendent Consciousness.

Just “number four”.

Turya is the underlying nondual consciousness that can be recognised throughout waking, dreaming and deep sleep.

And this is where the teaching becomes extraordinarily subtle.

Because turya is not simply another mental state to be entered after the other three.

It is the conscious reality that remains fundamental regardless of their changing contents.

In waking, experiences appear through sensory cognition.

In dreaming, experiences appear through mental constructions.

In deep sleep, differentiated cognition is unmanifest.

But from the standpoint of Trika, the ultimate reality of consciousness is not exhausted by any of these states.

The accomplished yogi recognises that reality through their transformations.

Imagine three films being projected onto a screen.

An action film.

A romantic comedy.

And an entirely black film in which nothing happens.

The images differ.

But the screen is not destroyed whenever the film changes.

Now take that metaphor carefully: unlike an ordinary screen, Śiva is not an inert support. Śiva is self-aware and free.

The point is the continuity of the underlying reality, not an assertion that your personal waking mind remains fully alert throughout every period of sleep.

The remarkable claim: Liberation does not require fleeing from ordinary conscious life. The same fundamental reality can be recognised through every mode of experience.
