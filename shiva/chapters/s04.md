# Śiva Sūtras 1.4

ज्ञानाधिष्ठानं मातृका

Jñānādhiṣṭhānaṃ mātṛkā.

Mātṛkā is the foundation of knowledge.

Your Alphabet Is Secretly Running the Show.

Now things get properly tantric.

Mātṛkā means the little mother, or mother principle.

Here she is the power associated with the Sanskrit phonemes — the letters from a through kṣa — as the basis of differentiated cognition.

This isn’t just a bizarre claim that the alphabet invented your suffering.

The idea is much more sophisticated.

Language articulates experience.

Experience becomes conceptualised.

Conceptualisation helps establish distinctions.

Those distinctions become entangled with identity, memory, desire, aversion and the sense of being an individual subject surrounded by other things.

You perceive an event.

Then language arrives.

“This is terrible.”

“That person disrespected me.”

“I always fail.”

And an entire little world forms around the verbal construction.

You’ve transformed a passing occurrence into a personal cosmological crisis.

All before breakfast.

For Kṣemarāja, however, Mātṛkā is not intrinsically evil.

She is Śakti.

The very same power that generates limitation can, when recognised, become a means of liberation.

This is enormously important for understanding Trika’s fascination with Sanskrit phonemes, mantra, and the metaphysics of speech.

Speech isn’t just a tool for describing an independently established universe.

Within this tradition, it belongs to the very process by which consciousness manifests differentiated reality.

The mother who makes the prison is also the mother who holds the key.

Rather inconvenient for the prison authorities, who are also Śiva.
