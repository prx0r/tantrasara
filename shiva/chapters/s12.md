# Śiva Sūtras 1.12

विस्मयो योगभूमिकाः

Vismayo yogabhūmikāḥ.

The stages of yoga are wonder.

Enlightenment Is Apparently Going “WHAT THE ACTUAL HELL” Forever.

This is absolutely gorgeous.

Vismaya means wonder, astonishment, amazement.

Yoga is union or spiritual integration.

Bhūmikāḥ are stages, levels, or grounds.

Now you might expect Vasugupta to give us a complicated hierarchy.

Level one: mild bliss.

Level two: unusual forehead sensations.

Level three: a very impressive dream about serpents.

Level four: you start wearing white linen.

No.

He says wonder.

Kṣemarāja explains that the extraordinary yogi encounters ever-renewed astonishment in the manifestation of reality.

Not because everything is mysterious in the sense of being incomprehensible.

But because consciousness continually reveals its own extraordinary nature.

What previously appeared mundane becomes marvellous.

A sound.

A colour.

The sensation of breathing.

The manifestation of a thought.

Each is an expression of the same inexhaustible reality.

The yogi discovers something rather surprising.

Recognition does not necessarily make the world less interesting.

It makes the world infinitely more astonishing.

Because the object is no longer merely an object.

It is an expression of Śakti.

And this is not wonder that depends on finding something new and exotic.

It’s wonder at what has been right in front of you the entire time.

Imagine a man studying a lamp.

He understands the electricity.

He understands the circuitry.

He understands the filament.

But suddenly there is an astonishment at the very fact that anything whatsoever manifests.

Not merely, “How does this device work?”

But, “What is this astonishing reality in which the device, the observer, and the act of knowing appear?”

The answer isn’t conceptual bewilderment.

It’s camatkāra — the aesthetic and experiential astonishment so beloved in the Kashmiri tradition.

Kṣemarāja even stresses that these stages are not simply a checklist of unusual yogic sensations. They concern the ever-fresh wonder of resting in one’s own conscious nature. Sanskrit & Trika Shaivism.

The universe has not become boring.

You have finally stopped taking its appearance for granted.
